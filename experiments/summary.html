A hidden pluck patch was rendered once (A2, held 0.6 s). Two players then tried to rebuild it from that audio alone. CMA-ES was told the patch structure and given six free knobs and 900 renders, but it never sees anything except a single distance number. Claude (a separate agent that had no access to the answer) had no structure hint and only 12 renders. It worked from the listening reports, the spectrogram sheets and the distance.

What this shows: the model got the kind of sound right on its first try (pitch, saw source, plucky shape) and worked out the 24 dB slope from a detail in the spectrogram image, not from the text report. The optimiser got the numbers closer but was handed the structure. Both hit the same ambiguity: cutoff, envelope depth and envelope decay trade off against each other, so several patches sound nearly the same. Claude's lab notebook is in experiments/llm-blind-match.md. It asks for new ears: per-harmonic decay times, a target-minus-candidate difference map, and the distance broken down by band.
